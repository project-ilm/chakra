Vyāsa records specific celestial states around the war. Researchers turn those verses into data points and search for a year whose sky matches. This page does exactly that on CHAKRA's own ephemeris — and, just as importantly, shows you which anchors can actually discriminate a date and where the instrument stops being trustworthy. It does not settle the question, and says so.

Read this first. CHAKRA is a heritage-computation instrument, not a deep-time ephemeris. Its elements are fitted near the present era; five millennia back, positional error grows from arc-minutes to degrees, and ΔT itself is uncertain by hours. Competing published dates (3067 BCE, 5561 BCE and others) differ mainly on how the Sanskrit is read, not on arithmetic. Nothing here is evidence for or against any date.

honesty Which anchors can actually date anything?

A constraint satisfied by most years cannot discriminate a year. This measures each anchor's hit rate across a sample so you can see, rather than assume, how much any single omen is worth. It is the reason serious chronology work uses ~140 observations rather than seven.
